Our customers are two groups in one place. Restaurants near McMaster that throw out unsold food and cancelled orders every night, and McMaster students who want a full meal they can afford. This isn't our opinion. We spoke to 10 local restaurants, and all of them called food waste a real problem. And 50 McMaster students told us they would buy cheaper food.

Too Good To Go already sells surprise bags. We do something it doesn't: you see the exact meal, and live bidding sets the price, which suits cancelled orders that have to move in minutes. AI matters here because staff have seconds, not minutes. They snap a photo and say a few words, and Gemini writes the listing. AI never decides allergens: staff confirm them every time. And a missed pickup goes back up for auction only after staff check it's still safe. [Switch to the app for the live demo, about 60 seconds] Let's show you, live. One thing to disclose first: payments run in Stripe test mode, and auction timers are shortened to one minute. I'm the restaurant. I photograph this dish and describe it out loud, and the listing appears with a title, a description and a starting price. I confirm the allergens and post it. On the student's phone, it's live. I bid, my teammate outbids me, and I get the alert. The timer ends, the winner is charged, and the pickup QR code is ready. I scan it, and the restaurant's sale shows up in its dashboard. Who pays? Both sides, a little. On a 10 dollar meal, the restaurant keeps 8 dollars, and we take a 20 percent commission plus a 50 cent buyer fee. That makes sense because the alternative for the restaurant is zero. After card fees we keep about 1.90. Running costs at pilot scale are about 85 to 105 dollars a month, including Gemini at 10 to 30 dollars for photo, voice and text. As volume grows, we add subscriptions and boosts that don't pay the card fee.
